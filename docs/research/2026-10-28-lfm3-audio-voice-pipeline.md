# LFM3-Audio in the voice pipeline: what wiring it in would take

**Date:** 2026-10-28 · **Scope:** LFM3-Audio-2B (the `LiquidAI/LFM3-Audio-2B-MLX-8bit` and `-4bit` packs) as the Mac's speech recognizer behind `SpeechRecognizer` and as its voice behind `SpeechSynthesizing`. Not the phone, and not a speech-to-speech Companion, which the 2026-10-27 LFM3 note covers.
**Method:** Read the vendored fork at `c27b315`, the port (`08c1d2e`) plus the text-only fix (the three LFM3-Audio files, `LFM3AudioTests.swift`, `tools/generate_lfm3_fixtures.py`), the app's speech seams, model catalog, Model Residency and LLM Gate code, the speech ADRs and their `CONTEXT.md` terms, the 4-bit pack's card and configs as downloaded (`~/Library/Application Support/models/LiquidAI_LFM3-Audio-2B-MLX-4bit`), and the 2026-10-27 note (it sits in the main checkout: `docs/research/` matches the `research/` ignore rule). Nothing was built, run or measured. Speeds are the fork's or Liquid's. Every other number says it is a guess or an estimate.
**Measured since:** [2026-10-28-lfm3-audio-measured.md](2026-10-28-lfm3-audio-measured.md) ran §6's measurements on public-domain clips. Accuracy came out a point behind Whisper, and a take released during a 27B prefill in the app's process waited 25–28 s (about 2 s in its own process). The voice is as fast as Qwen3-TTS, pending a blind listen.

---

## In short

- **ASR fits the existing port with one new adapter.** The fork takes speech through the normal generate path: a chat message carries the clip, the processor turns it into log-mel features and `<|audio|>` placeholders, and greedy text comes out with the speech markers suppressed. An `LFM3AudioSpeechRecognizer` resamples the take to 16 kHz, asks `Perform ASR in English.`, and returns text with no segments.
- **The code around the port assumes Whisper.** The `.mlmodelc` contract at load and at launch, a recognizer factory that only makes WhisperKit, a picker and a selection rule keyed to the Speech-to-Text category, and a Live Preview that confirms words at Whisper's segment boundaries. Each needs a small change. Without segments the preview still runs, but it re-decodes up to 20 s each cycle and confirms nothing before that.
- **For ASR, latency under load is the first unknown and accuracy the second.** Whisper runs in Core ML, outside MLX's process-wide lock. LFM3 runs in MLX, so a paste could wait behind a 27B prefill chunk of about 5 s. Accuracy on the owner's technical English is unmeasured, and the Learned Words were taught by Whisper's mistakes.
- **TTS fits the port too, at a product cost.** The codec's frames are the engine's frames (24 kHz, 1,920 samples), the streamed decode adds up to the whole one, and cancellation lands within a frame. But the voice becomes a generic female or male per locale: no VoiceDesign, no Reference Take, no Pinned Voice, so Jarvis loses his voice. There is no Alignment Head, so the Read-Along falls back to spreading characters, a median 504 ms off. Whether "female" stays the same person from segment to segment is unknown.
- **The TTS adapter belongs in a new package target.** `LFM3Audio` lives in `MLXVLM`, and the phone links `TesseractSpeech` but not `MLXVLM`.
- **Do not share the agent's load.** It works in principle, but it breaks ADR-0081's safety argument, the arbiter's ownership and Model Residency's numbers, and it only matters when the agent is LFM3-Audio too. Later, let the recognizer and the synthesizer share one speech-side load: about 2.5 GB at 8 bits. If it replaced both, today's pair is about 1.5 GB for Whisper Turbo plus 2.88 GB peak for Qwen3-TTS.
- **Catalog:** one entry per checkpoint with speech roles, not duplicate entries on one folder, and a completeness rule that requires `audio_tokenizer/`. Pick the recognizer in Settings → Dictation. Settings → Speech needs a voice engine choice, which does not exist today.
- **Order:** measure first, with no app code (accuracy replay, latency during a prefill, a blind listen). Then ASR for dictation, opt-in. Then the synthesizer in its own target, measured in `v2-listen`, before any app wiring. A shared speech-side load and live ASR come last.

## 1. What the fork gives

- **Speech in.** Chat messages and `UserInput` carry `audios` ([UserInput.swift:258-281](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/UserInput.swift#L258-L281), [:430](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/UserInput.swift#L430)). `LFM3AudioProcessor.prepare(input:)` renders the template with each clip as an audio part before the text, repeats `<|audio|>` once per encoder token (8 feature frames, 80 ms), and returns an `LMInput` whose `audio` holds `[clips, 128, frames]` features and a mask ([LFM3Audio.swift:594-624](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L594-L624), [:648-666](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L648-L666), [:52-55](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L52-L55)). An `.array` clip must already be 16 kHz mono; a `.url` clip is resampled ([:573-592](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L573-L592)). One prompt at a time ([:456-460](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L456-L460)).
- **Text out.** Through the standard `LanguageModel` path, `LFM3Audio.prepare` scatters the projected speech features into the placeholders and prefills, and the model never writes a speech marker ([LFM3Audio.swift:396-401](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L396-L401), [:428-432](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L428-L432), [:479-491](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L479-L491)). It is registered as `lfm3_audio` with `Lfm3AudioProcessor`, so `VLMModelFactory` loads it like any VLM ([VLMModelFactory.swift:112](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift#L112), [VLMProcessorLoadingRegistry.swift:77](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMProcessorLoadingRegistry.swift#L77)).
- **Speech out.** `LFM3AudioGenerator` is a synchronous `Sequence` of slots: `.text(token)`, `.speech(codes)` with 12 codes per 80 ms frame, and `.endOfSpeech` ([LFM3AudioGeneration.swift:44-50](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L44-L50), [:197-266](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L197-L266)). It takes an `LMInput`, an output mode (`.speech` appends `<|audio_start|>`, `.interleaved` appends `<|mixed_start|>`), the card's speech sampling and `maxSlots` 2,048. It prefills in `init`, holds its own cache and is not thread-safe ([:11-41](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L11-L41), [:52-58](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L52-L58), [:93-143](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L93-L143)).
- **Codec.** `LFM3AudioCodec.load(modelDirectory:)` reads `audio_tokenizer/`, or `audio_detokenizer/` in older packs ([LFM3AudioCodec.swift:176-206](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioCodec.swift#L176-L206)). `makeStream()` returns a stream with its own cache. Its `decode(_:isFinal:)` returns 24 kHz float32 samples that no later frame changes and holds back 960 samples until the next call; the final call flushes them, so the pieces add up to the whole decode, 1,920 samples a frame ([:262-334](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioCodec.swift#L262-L334), tested at [LFM3AudioTests.swift:284-313](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L284-L313)). Codes must be below 2,016, so end-of-speech frames are dropped first ([LFM3AudioCodec.swift:222-234](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioCodec.swift#L222-L234)).
- **Interleaving.** `.interleaved` alternates 6 text tokens and 12 frames (0.96 s), then speaks the rest after `<|text_end|>` ([LFM3AudioGeneration.swift:227-236](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L227-L236), [:252-258](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L252-L258), [LFM3Audio.swift:764-766](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L764-L766)). The text is the model's own answer, so this serves a spoken chat, not reading text it is given.
- **Not there.** No streaming audio input: the encoder reads a whole clip ([LFM3Audio.swift:188-189](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L188-L189), [:292-295](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L292-L295)), so no live ASR. No way to continue earlier speech output: only `<|audio|>` placeholders take embeddings ([:455-477](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3Audio.swift#L455-L477)). No alignment output. The sampler draws from MLX's ambient random state ([LFM3AudioGeneration.swift:166](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L166), [:172-174](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L172-L174)).
- **Checked.** The fork's commit (`08c1d2e`) says float32 matches mlx-vlm on LiquidAI's 4-bit pack, with identical greedy text and codes. The opt-in parity test feeds the fixture's token ids directly ([LFM3AudioTests.swift:385-387](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L385-L387), [:427-433](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L427-L433)), so prompts built by `LFM3AudioProcessor` are not parity-checked. The fixture's prompts are the card's: `Perform ASR in English.` and `Perform TTS in American English using a female voice.` ([generate_lfm3_fixtures.py:150-158](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/tools/generate_lfm3_fixtures.py#L150-L158), [:177-186](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/tools/generate_lfm3_fixtures.py#L177-L186)).

## 2. ASR: an `LFM3AudioSpeechRecognizer`

### 2.1 The adapter

- **Audio.** The port hands over `AudioData` at the device rate, mono float32 ([AudioData.swift:20-27](../../tesseract/Models/AudioData.swift#L20-L27), [AudioCaptureEngine.swift:606-619](../../tesseract/Core/Audio/AudioCaptureEngine.swift#L606-L619)). The adapter resamples to 16 kHz with `AudioConverter.resample`, as the Whisper adapter does ([WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift:69-78](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift#L69-L78)), and passes the clip as `.array`.
- **Prompt.** A system turn `Perform ASR in <language>.` and a user turn holding only the clip. The port gives an ISO code, or nil for auto ([SpeechRecognizer.swift:29-32](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/SpeechRecognizer.swift#L29-L32), [TranscriptionEngine.swift:175-176](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/TranscriptionEngine.swift#L175-L176)); dictation defaults to `en` ([SettingsCatalogue+Mac.swift:51](../../tesseract/Features/Settings/SettingsCatalogue+Mac.swift#L51)). The adapter maps codes to names and sends `Perform ASR.` for nil. The Audio template has no `enable_thinking`, and its generation prompt opens no `<think>` block (the pack's `chat_template.jinja`).
- **Decoding.** Greedy, at most 1,024 tokens ([model-parameters.md](../model-parameters.md), the `lfm3-audio` rows), on the adapter's own `ModelContainer` from `VLMModelFactory`: the normal generate path. `LFM3AudioGenerator(output: .text)` is the other way in. The fork tests the standard prefill's logits and the generator's ASR tokens ([LFM3AudioTests.swift:164-187](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L164-L187), [:388-425](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L388-L425)), not a whole decode through the standard path.
- **Result.** Text only. `segments` stays empty, `language` echoes the request (a guess for auto), and empty text throws `noSpeechDetected`, as Whisper's adapter does ([WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift:105-107](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift#L105-L107)).
- **Cancellation.** Task cancellation is the port's one channel ([SpeechRecognizer.swift:34-38](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/SpeechRecognizer.swift#L34-L38)). Check it per token. The encoder and the prefill are evaluations that can't stop midway (estimate: well under 0.2 s for a 20 s window).
- **Load.** Prewarm with a short silent clip, as Whisper prewarms ([WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift:50-57](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift#L50-L57)). Never set `Memory.cacheLimit`, which belongs to `LLMActor` ([ADR-0039](../adr/0039-tts-residency-lazy-load-keep-warm-no-cache-limit.md) §4, [LLMActor.swift:54](../../tesseract/Features/Agent/LLMActor.swift#L54)).

### 2.2 What changes around the port

- **The file contract.** `TranscriptionEngine.loadModel` refuses a folder without `AudioEncoder.mlmodelc` and `TextDecoder.mlmodelc` ([TranscriptionEngine.swift:88-95](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/TranscriptionEngine.swift#L88-L95), [WhisperModelContract.swift:16](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperModelContract.swift#L16)). App Bindings' model-path input applies the same rule ([DependencyContainer.swift:919-926](../../tesseract/App/DependencyContainer.swift#L919-L926)), and the port's own doc says `.mlmodelc` ([SpeechRecognizer.swift:26](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/SpeechRecognizer.swift#L26)). Each recognizer needs its own rule, still checked above the port ([ARCHITECTURE.md → Speech Seams](../../ARCHITECTURE.md), lines 403-409).
- **The factory.** `makeRecognizer` takes no input and defaults to WhisperKit ([TranscriptionEngine.swift:45](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/TranscriptionEngine.swift#L45), [:69-76](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/TranscriptionEngine.swift#L69-L76), [:101](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/TranscriptionEngine.swift#L101)). It has to choose by the selected model.
- **The selection.** The Dictation picker lists downloaded `.speechToText` entries ([DictationSettingsPane.swift:41-53](../../tesseract/Features/Settings/Panes/DictationSettingsPane.swift#L41-L53)). Launch resets any selection outside that category ([SettingsManager.swift:1100-1121](../../tesseract/Features/Settings/SettingsManager.swift#L1100-L1121)), and healing and auto-load key on the same id ([AppBindings.swift:183-196](../../tesseract/App/AppBindings.swift#L183-L196), [:201-237](../../tesseract/App/AppBindings.swift#L201-L237)).
- **Every surface switches at once.** Voice Capture gives every surface a session over one shared recognizer ([ADR-0096](../adr/0096-voice-capture-hands-every-surface-a-whole-session.md) §1, [VoiceCaptureSession.swift:427-431](../../tesseract/Core/VoiceCaptureSession.swift#L427-L431)): dictation, the composers, the panels and the voice session's ear. An ear for the voice session alone (2026-10-27 note §7.2) would need Voice Capture to hold two.
- **The text pipeline.** The regex cleanup and the Learned Words run on any text, but the Learned Words were taught by Whisper's mishearings. A recognizer that mishears differently fires them less ([dictation note](2026-10-03-dictation-streaming.md) §1.3). Correction Pairs record the recognizer, so takes stay comparable ([DictationCoordinator.swift:279-281](../../tesseract/Features/Dictation/DictationCoordinator.swift#L279-L281)).
- **Silence and noise.** Whisper drops windows below a no-speech threshold of 0.6 and chunks long takes by VAD ([WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift:16-18](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift#L16-L18), [:80-90](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift#L80-L90)). LFM3 has neither. The session still skips a silent capture ([VoiceCaptureSession.swift:443-444](../../tesseract/Core/VoiceCaptureSession.swift#L443-L444)); what LFM3 writes for noise is unknown.

### 2.3 Live Preview and live ASR

- Segments matter only to the Live Preview: `LivePreviewAssembler` is their one reader ([LivePreview.swift:64-97](../../tesseract/Features/Dictation/Lens/LivePreview.swift#L64-L97)), confirming all but the last two, which is WhisperKit's rule ([ADR-0086](../adr/0086-the-lens-streams-and-the-paste-stays-a-full-pass.md) lines 66-78). With no segments, every decode is tail: the window grows from the take's start, is decoded whole each cycle, and is settled at once past 20 s ([LivePreview.swift:69-80](../../tesseract/Features/Dictation/Lens/LivePreview.swift#L69-L80)). Until then the Lens shows every word dimmed, and the cut can split a word.
- The encoder re-reads the whole window each cycle. Estimate from the fork's speeds (prefill 3,640 tok/s, decode 228 tok/s at 4 bits, [model-parameters.md → LFM3](../model-parameters.md#lfm3-17b-vl-2b-audio-2b)): a 20 s window is 250 audio tokens and about 60 text tokens, about 0.35 s plus the encoder. That is near Whisper's GPU decode, and unmeasured.
- A confirmation rule of its own, keeping the words two decodes agree on, would end the dimming. That is a guess at a design; nothing like it is built.
- Live ASR (`Perform live ASR.`, about 0.96 s chunks, English only, a fresh text context every 30 s; 2026-10-27 note §1.2) needs a streaming encoder and incremental prefill in the fork. The paste stays a full pass either way (ADR-0086).

### 2.4 Latency

- Whisper runs through Core ML ([WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift:50-57](../../tesseract/Features/Transcription/WhisperKitSpeechRecognizer.swift#L50-L57)), outside MLX. MLX wraps every evaluation in one process-wide lock, and a 1,024-token 27B prefill chunk holds it about 5 s ([ADR-0081](../adr/0081-speech-never-waits-for-the-llm.md) lines 34-39, 79-85). LFM3 would wait behind it, where Whisper only slowed from 0.65 s to 1.45 s with the 27B decoding (dictation note §1.6, §1.7). Guess: a paste during a Companion moment's prefill could wait several seconds longer than today.
- Idle, the same speeds suggest a 10 s take (125 audio tokens, about 30 text tokens) decodes in about 0.2 s plus the encoder. An estimate, not a measurement.
- If the lock bites: run LFM3 in its own process (2026-10-27 note §5), fall back to Whisper while the LLM Gate is held (the Proofread Pass's skip-when-busy, [ADR-0034](../adr/0034-proofread-pass-own-mlx-model.md)), or wait for a stream per consumer, which is blocked on MLX 0.32 (ADR-0081 lines 93-94).

### 3.2 Streaming into the engine's frames

- Prompt: a system turn `Perform TTS in <locale> using a <female|male> voice.` and a user turn with the segment's text. `.speech` appends `<|audio_start|>` after the generation prompt, which is the card's prefill ([LFM3AudioGeneration.swift:110-127](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L110-L127)).
- Frames go to `stream.decode` through `LFM3AudioCodec.codes(frames:)` ([LFM3AudioGeneration.swift:269-275](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L269-L275)); `.endOfSpeech` ends the segment with an empty final call. The first call returns 1,920·n − 480 samples, later calls 1,920·n, the final call the rest ([LFM3AudioCodec.swift:287-328](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioCodec.swift#L287-L328)). The engine counts frames from cumulative samples, so uneven chunks are fine ([SpeechEngine.swift:303-311](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/SpeechEngine.swift#L303-L311)).
- Decode the first frame alone for a quick first sound, then about 5 frames at a time (0.4 s, Qwen's interval, [Qwen3Synthesizer.swift:271-274](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Qwen3Synthesizer.swift#L271-L274)). A guess to tune. In bf16 the streamed audio stays within 2% of a whole decode ([LFM3AudioTests.swift:472-474](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L472-L474)).
- Cap frames by the text's length as well as `maxTokens` ([Values.swift:185](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L185)), so a model that never ends its speech can't run on; segments are about 100 tokens ([Segmenter.swift:14](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Segmenter.swift#L14)), and `maxSlots` 2,048 is 164 s.
- First audio is unmeasured. Estimate: the prefill, one frame at 104 frames/s and one codec call, tens of milliseconds. Qwen's is about 54 ms ([ADR-0097](../adr/0097-the-reply-speaks-as-it-is-written-and-a-short-turn-ends-sooner.md) line 21).
- `SilenceCap`, which trims long silences in Qwen's adapter ([Qwen3Synthesizer.swift:277-279](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Qwen3Synthesizer.swift#L277-L279)), is internal to `TesseractSpeech` ([SilenceCap.swift:11](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/SilenceCap.swift#L11)).

### 3.3 Cancellation and seeds

- The generator is synchronous. Checking `Task.checkCancellation()` per slot stops within one frame, about 10 ms at 104 frames/s, which meets the port's "within one decoder step" ([Ports.swift:100-103](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Ports.swift#L100-L103)).
- The engine resolves one seed per utterance ([SpeechEngine.swift:213-217](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/SpeechEngine.swift#L213-L217)). Run each segment inside `withRandomState(RandomState(seed:))`, a task-local in the pinned mlx-swift (`3425495`, `Source/MLX/State.swift:99-104`), so the seed applies and no other model's sampling moves.

### 3.4 Sampling against `TTSParameters`

- `TTSParameters` is Qwen's: temperature 0.9, top-p 1.0, repetition penalty 1.05, detail temperature 0.5, `maxTokens` 4,096 ([Values.swift:173-193](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L173-L193)). `LFM3AudioSampling` is the card's: temperature 1.0, top-k 128, repetition penalty 1.1 over 4,096 frames ([LFM3AudioGeneration.swift:25-41](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioGeneration.swift#L25-L41), the pack's `generation_config.json`). Top-p and detail temperature have no counterpart; top-k and the window have no field.
- Use the card's sampling and read only `maxTokens` until someone has listened. Hide the Settings → Speech sampler while LFM3 speaks.

### 3.5 Voices

- A voice is `Voice.preset(speaker:language:)` ([Values.swift:86](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L86)), which never takes a Reference Take ([SpeechEngine.swift:206-208](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/SpeechEngine.swift#L206-L208)). The Mac has no preset setting (`presetVoice` is nil, [SpeechSettings.swift:25-32](../../tesseract/Features/Speech/SpeechSettings.swift#L25-L32)), its voice source is `.designed` ([ModelDefinition.swift:165](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L165)), and the designer shows only for a designed source ([VoicesSheet.swift:71](../../tesseract/Features/Speech/Voices/VoicesSheet.swift#L71)).
- Locales: map `TTSLanguage` ([TTSLanguage.swift:8-18](../../tesseract/Models/TTSLanguage.swift#L8-L18)) to the card's names. English becomes `American English`, the fixture's string; the other names are a guess to check against the card. Russian has no LFM3 voice.
- Lost: VoiceDesign, the Reference Take and "Try another take" ([ADR-0072](../adr/0072-reference-take-keeps-a-designed-voice.md)), every Pinned Voice (fingerprinted per checkpoint, [Values.swift:110-161](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L110-L161)), and with them Jarvis's designed voice.
- Unknown: whether "female" is one person from segment to segment. The fork can't continue earlier speech (§1, "Not there"). VoiceDesign drifted exactly so before ADR-0072, and a reply spoken in rounds as it is written ([ADR-0097](../adr/0097-the-reply-speaks-as-it-is-written-and-a-short-turn-ends-sooner.md)) would make a drift audible inside one answer.

### 3.6 Read-Along and word timing

- No Alignment Head has been measured, so `alignmentHead` is nil and no `.words` arrive ([Values.swift:11-14](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L11-L14), [:372-374](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L372-L374); [ADR-0077](../adr/0077-words-are-timed-by-the-voice-models-own-attention.md) lines 38-39). The Read-Along then spreads each segment's characters over its audio (ADR-0077 lines 59-61; `CONTEXT.md` → **Read-Along**): words start a median 504 ms off, and 21% land within 200 ms (ADR-0077 lines 22-23). The Reader and the Speech Overlay follow that clock.
- The owner ruled out a recognizer beside the voice (ADR-0077 line 28). LFM3's six global-attention layers may hold a head that follows the prompt text while frames are written. A guess that would need ADR-0077's measurement again.

### 3.7 Where the code goes

- `LFM3Audio` and its codec live in `MLXVLM`. `TesseractSpeech` depends only on `Qwen3TTS` and MLX ([Package.swift:37-44](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Package.swift#L37-L44)), and the phone links `TesseractSpeech` but not `MLXVLM`, which the Mac links ([project.pbxproj:395](../../tesseract.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj#L395), [:402](../../tesseract.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj#L402), [:473-477](../../tesseract.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj#L473-L477)). So the adapter goes in a new target and product, say `LFM3Speech`, which the Mac app and `v2-listen` link. `LFM3AudioCheckpoint` is disk-only and stays in `TesseractSpeech` beside `Qwen3Checkpoint`, because the catalog imports that package and is shared with the phone ([ModelDefinition.swift:7](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L7); `CONTEXT.md` → **Usable Directory**).
- `TTSModelSpec.Precision` has q6, q8 and bf16 ([Values.swift:16-18](../../Vendor/tesseract-speech/Sources/TesseractSpeech/Values.swift#L16-L18)). The 8-bit pack fits; the 4-bit one needs a `.q4`.
- The presenter holds one engine for good (`let engine`, [SpeechEnginePresenter.swift:27](../../tesseract/Features/Speech/SpeechEnginePresenter.swift#L27)), built from the static spec ([DependencyContainer.swift:526-545](../../tesseract/App/DependencyContainer.swift#L526-L545)), and the coordinator looks up Pinned Voices by that spec ([SpeechCoordinator.swift:372](../../tesseract/Features/Speech/SpeechCoordinator.swift#L372)). Choosing a voice engine needs an engine rebuild or a relaunch.

## 4. One load or two

- **Today it would be two.** An LFM3-Audio agent is the `LLMActor`'s private container ([LLMActor.swift:62](../../tesseract/Features/Agent/LLMActor.swift#L62), [:186-199](../../tesseract/Features/Agent/LLMActor.swift#L186-L199)). A speech side loads its own `LFM3Audio`, plus the codec for TTS.
- **Sharing works in principle.** Each generator keeps its own cache, and mlx-swift-lm already reads evaluated weights concurrently after a prefill ([ModelContainer.swift:211-216](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/ModelContainer.swift#L211-L216)). Against it:
  - The model can't leave the container cleanly. `perform` returns only `Sendable` values ([ModelContainer.swift:90-96](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/ModelContainer.swift#L90-L96)), an MLXNN `Module` is not one (mlx-swift `3425495`, `Source/MLXNN/Module.swift:97`), and `perform` holds the container's lock for its whole body ([SerialAccessContainer.swift:53-58](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/Utilities/SerialAccessContainer.swift#L53-L58)). Speech would run inside that lock, or behind an unchecked box.
  - ADR-0081's case for models side by side assumes "consumers share no arrays" (lines 34-39). Speech and dictation never take the LLM Gate (lines 59-64), so they would read weights a gate holder is using.
  - The arbiter unloads and swaps that model under the gate ([InferenceArbiter.swift:151-175](../../tesseract/Features/Agent/InferenceArbiter.swift#L151-L175), [:191-208](../../tesseract/Features/Agent/InferenceArbiter.swift#L191-L208)). A speech reference would keep the weights alive after Offload Model, and Model Residency would report memory freed that isn't.
  - The agent load stacks projections in place (`stackTargetProjections`, [LLMActor.swift:243-256](../../tesseract/Features/Agent/LLMActor.swift#L243-L256)), but only in modules that opt in, and no LFM3 module does ([SameInputProjectionStacking.swift:15-35](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXLLM/SameInputProjectionStacking.swift#L15-L35)).
  - It matters only when the agent is LFM3-Audio too. The owner's agent is Qwen3.8-27B (2026-10-27 note §2).
- **Memory per load.** An MLX model holds about its weights' size on disk ([ModelResidency.swift:14-15](../../tesseract/App/ModelResidency.swift#L14-L15)). 4-bit: 1.58 GB plus a 0.15 GB codec (the pack's card). 8-bit: about 2.5 GB ([ModelDefinition.swift:478](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L478)). The KV cache is 12 KB a token ([model-parameters.md → LFM3](../model-parameters.md#lfm3-17b-vl-2b-audio-2b)), about 15 MB for a 60 s take (estimate). Today Whisper Turbo is about 1.5 GB ([ModelDefinition.swift:189](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L189)) and Qwen3-TTS q6 peaks at 2.88 GB ([:136-139](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L136-L139)). Guess: one 8-bit speech load serving both roles takes about 2.5 GB where those two take about 4.4 GB. Peak RSS, ADR-0039's metric, is unmeasured.
- **Recommendation.** The agent keeps its load. Later, the recognizer and the synthesizer share one speech-side load, at 8 bits: ASR may tolerate 4, but speech wants 8 (mlx-vlm kept 68% of float32's speech codes at 4 bits; 2026-10-27 note §6).
- **Model Residency.** The Voice row names the fixed Voice Engine id ([DependencyContainer+ModelResidency.swift:60-72](../../tesseract/App/DependencyContainer+ModelResidency.swift#L60-L72)). The Dictation row has no size and no unload, because Whisper is always armed and runs "in its own memory" ([:74-96](../../tesseract/App/DependencyContainer+ModelResidency.swift#L74-L96)). An MLX recognizer needs a size, and the owner has to decide whether Offload may free 2.5 GB of always-armed dictation. A shared load needs one size for two rows; today each row adds its own ([ModelResidency.swift:66-74](../../tesseract/App/ModelResidency.swift#L66-L74)).

## 5. Catalog and Settings

- **Today.** Both LFM3-Audio entries are `.agent` with the default completeness rule ([ModelDefinition.swift:468-495](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L468-L495)), and an entry has exactly one category ([:67](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L67)).
- **Duplicate entries per role would share one folder** by the folder rule ([ModelDefinition.swift:92-101](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L92-L101)), and that breaks: delete removes the folder but resets one entry's status ([ModelDownloadManager.swift:447-453](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDownloadManager.swift#L447-L453)); the download guard is per id, so two entries can fill one folder at once ([:194](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDownloadManager.swift#L194), [:213](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDownloadManager.swift#L213)); the cache total counts the folder once per entry ([:478-482](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDownloadManager.swift#L478-L482)); the Models page lists it once per category ([ModelsPageView.swift:23](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelsPageView.swift#L23)).
- **Recommendation: one entry with speech roles** (recognizer, synthesizer) beside its category. The Dictation picker, launch normalization and healing list by role; a new Speech setting does the same for synthesizers. A guess at the shape; the owner decides.
- **Completeness: `.lfm3AudioCheckpoint`.** The rule would require `config.json`, every shard the index names, `tokenizer.json` and `tokenizer_config.json`, `chat_template.jinja`, `preprocessor_config.json`, and `audio_tokenizer/config.json` with its weights (the codec also takes `audio_detokenizer/`, [LFM3AudioCodec.swift:176-191](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Libraries/MLXVLM/Models/LFM3AudioCodec.swift#L176-L191)). Under today's rule a folder holding only `audio_tokenizer/model.safetensors` counts as downloaded, the bug ADR-0039's amendment fixed for Qwen (ADR-0039 lines 74-77). The agent entries gain from the fix too.
- **Settings.** Dictation → Model lists every recognizer, Whisper first, and the language picker narrows when LFM3 is picked. Speech gains a voice engine choice; no TTS selection setting exists, only the fixed id and spec ([ModelDefinition.swift:130](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L130), [:139](../../tesseract/Features/Models/ModelDefinition.swift#L139)). With LFM3 picked, the voices sheet shows the two presets, the languages narrow and the sampler hides.

## 6. Slices

**Before any app code: measure** (`CLAUDE.md`, smallest harness first), with a throwaway Swift tool on the fork. Stop if step 1 or 3 fails. Done on 2026-10-28 without the owner's takes, so step 1 still lacks its Capture Dump replay: [the measurements](2026-10-28-lfm3-audio-measured.md).

1. Accuracy: LFM3 ASR on the latest 100 Capture Dump takes at 8 and 4 bits, scored as the 2026-10-03 note did (against Whisper's text and the Correction Pairs, technical words apart). Transcripts stay outside the repository.
2. Latency: release-to-text and one preview decode, with the 27B idle and while it prefills a 1,024-token chunk, ABAB.
3. Listening: ten Companion lines and one multi-segment passage in each voice at 8 bits, blind against the Pinned Voice, noting any change of speaker between segments.

**Slice 1, the minimal one: LFM3 ASR for dictation, opt-in.** The recognizer adapter; a file contract per recognizer at load and in App Bindings; the factory choosing by the selected model; speech roles and `.lfm3AudioCheckpoint` in the catalog; the Dictation picker by role; a size on the Dictation row. Whisper stays the default, and the Live Preview runs as §2.3 says. The engine's tests already run over the in-memory recognizer peer; the adapter gets a tiny random-model test like the fork's ([LFM3AudioTests.swift:15-55](../../Vendor/mlx-swift-lm/Tests/MLXLMTests/LFM3AudioTests.swift#L15-L55)).

**Slice 2: the synthesizer in `LFM3Speech`,** driven by a `v2-listen` mode for first audio, real-time factor and peak RSS. No app wiring.

**Slice 3: TTS in the app.** The voice engine setting with an engine rebuild, the presets and the locale map, the Voice row following the setting, the sampler hidden. The Read-Along runs untimed.

**Slice 4: one speech-side load** for the recognizer and the synthesizer, with one size in Model Residency.

**Slice 5: live ASR in the fork** (the streaming encoder and `Perform live ASR.`), and a Live Preview rule that needs no segments. English only.

A speech-to-speech talker on interleaved output is a separate design (2026-10-27 note §7.3).
